# My Learning About What Makes AI Research More Valuable: Preparing PhD Application

The value of AI research cannot necessarily be characterized along a single dimension as far as I learned, such as methodological novelty or performance improvement. Rather, meaningful research contributions can emerge at different stages of the research process—from identifying a problem to developing methods, establishing empirical evidence, and generating new knowledge. From this perspective, I summarized 4 complementary dimensions of research contribution that I have learned (this would be improved as I learn more): <br>

Problem formulation value: Research can contribute by identifying, formulating, or reframing problems that are important yet insufficiently explored. The value of a problem formulation may arise from a combination of its long-term sustainability as a research problem, practical significance, and the degree of under-exploration (finding new under-explored tasks may require deeper domain knowledges). A valuable contribution does not always require inventing an entirely new task (but this may increase the contribution of the research), revealing an overlooked aspect of an existing problem or providing a formulation that enables systematic analysis. <br>

Methodological value: Research may contribute through novel and sound approaches to solving or understanding a problem. Such contributions can arise at multiple levels, including algorithms, network architectures, pipelines, loss functions, evaluation metrics, dataset construction, learning paradigms, and conceptual or theoretical frameworks toward existing tasks or problems. <br>

Experimental value: Experiments can constitute a substantive research contribution when they provide strong and reliable empirical evidence beyond demonstrating isolated performance gains. This value may include improvements in performance, robustness, generalizability across datasets, domains (the domain can be represented as datasets as well), tasks, and experimental settings, as well as systematic identification of failure cases and fine-grained analyses of the proposed methods. Such evidence can clarify not only whether an approach works, but also when, where, and why it succeeds or fails. <br>

Value of findings: AI research can also generate new knowledge beyond performance improvement on established tasks. Such findings may reveal previously unknown properties, behaviors, limitations, or produce new scientific, medical, mathematical, or domain-specific insights.<br>

These dimensions are complementary rather than mutually exclusive as far as I learned. A single study may contribute along several dimensions—for example, by formulating an under-explored problem, proposing a new methodology, demonstrating its generalizability, and revealing previously unknown properties of AI systems. Preparing PhD application and beyond, I hope I can deepen my understanding about better research values to contribute for the better society. <br>
